# What kids (9–13) and parents say in reviews about horse games — sentiment synthesis

Research date: 2026-11-02. Scope: review text about mobile horse games (plus the mobile port of Star Stable Online), separated into kids' voices, parents' voices and riders' voices, feeding the design of a browser co-op (2–4 player) show-jumping game for two siblings aged 10–11 who ride and jump for real.

**Evidence base actually read (all quotes below are verbatim fragments from these):**
- **Apple App Store customer-review RSS feeds** (primary source), pulled 2026-11-02 for 21 horse apps, storefronts us/gb/au/ca (3 most-recent pages each) + de/ru/fr/nl/se (1–2 pages): 11,770 reviews total, 6,619 in English, 4,327 of those dated 2024 or later. Because feeds are "most recent", big apps are skewed to 2025–2026; small apps reach back to 2017–2021 (flagged where used). Listing pages: [Equestrian the Game](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1468871996), [Rival Stars Horse Racing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1215477266), [Star Stable Online: Horse Game](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1445695988), [Horse Riding Tales](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1442304261), [Wildshade Fantasy Horse Races](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1588707549), [Horse Life (Ketchapp)](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1660947900), [Equestriad World Tour 26](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1532186208), [Horse World – Show Jumping](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1162693632), [Jumping Horses Champions 3](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1180636312), [My Horse](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id421167112), [Star Equestrian – Horse Ranch](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1635888697), [Star Stable: Horses](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1126342383), [Horse Games EverRun](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1113619083). ## Q1. Common Sense Media: what kid and parent reviews say about Star Stable, Rival Stars and the others

### Takeaway
Only Star Stable (528 kid / 213 parent reviews) and Rival Stars (3 kid / 2 parent) have CSM user-review pages; Equestrian the Game, Horse Riding Tales, My Riding Stables, Horse Club Adventures and Wildshade have no CSM page at all. Kids love Star Stable's world, graphics, quests and friends-with-a-club feeling but hate the paywall at level 5, $18–40 horses and a chat where "someone has to be a bully"; parents' reviews converge on cost, "predatory" purchasing, bullying/grooming risk and bad customer support.

### Inferences
- For kids the retention engine of Star Stable is social (friend calls, clubs, role-play) + a steady drip of new content; the churn engine is the level-5 paywall and FOMO pricing. Their complaints are rarely about riding mechanics — several say the riding/racing is "too easy" or "boring robots".
- Parents judge a horse game mainly on (1) whether it nags the child to spend, (2) stranger chat. "Educational"/horse-care value is the main positive they mention.
- Rival Stars' few CSM kid reviews praise exactly what Star Stable kids miss: skill-based play ("not a clicker game", "real to life").

### Gaps
- No CSM pages exist (404 / site search returns only movies) for Equestrian the Game, Horse Riding Tales, My Riding Stables, Horse Club Adventures or Wildshade, so no CSM kid/parent quotes for them.
- CSM parent reviews are sorted "most helpful" and the ones visible are 2021–2023; recent (2024–2026) parent voices had to come from App Store reviews instead (Q2/Q3).

## Q2. App Store / Google Play: most common 1–2★ complaints and 5★ praises per game

### Takeaway
Across ~4,300 English reviews from 2024–2026 the 1–2★ complaints are dominated by money (paywalls, VIP/membership, horses sold for real cash) and ads, then crashes/lost progress, then timers/energy; genuine control complaints cluster in the jumping games (Equestrian the Game's swipe-reins, Horse World's tilt steering, Jumping Horses Champions' timing). 5★ praise is about realistic-looking horses, "you can actually do stuff with your horse" (breed, jump, cross-country, trail), and friends.

### Inferences (design implications for a 2–4 player browser show-jumping game for two 10–11-year-old riders)
- The strongest differentiators available, judged by review pain: (a) jumping that is skill-based *and* legible (visible take-off zone / stride cue, refusals only for real reasons, poles that fall for a reason), (b) no energy/timers — unlimited rounds, (c) friends in the same arena with invite-only presence and preset-phrase or no chat, (d) zero ads and no real-money horses, (e) one-thumb controls that never require tilting the iPad.
- Because the players are riders, invest in correct canter leads, rider seat/posture over the fence, fence variety (oxers, verticals, liverpools) and course design; let them recreate their own horse (coat, markings, name).
- Co-op scope that matches what kids ask for: ride side by side in a warm-up field, then jump the same course in turn or simultaneously with ghost/side-by-side comparison, plus a silly mini-game; a shared stable is not something anyone asked for.
