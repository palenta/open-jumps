# Co-op design patterns (2–4 players) and real equestrian team formats — transfer to a browser show-jumping game for kids

Research notes, 2 Nov 2026. Context: browser co-op (2–4 players) show-jumping game for 10–11-year-olds who ride real horses; phones/iPads, each on own device, often in the same room; short sessions. Source dates are marked where known. Tooling note: web-search budget was exhausted mid-research, so Reddit sentiment threads and a few specific rulebooks (Hickstead relay, pas de deux) could not be fetched — see Gaps.

---

## Key Question 1 — Which co-op mechanics work best for 2–4 players of mixed skill, on separate touch devices, in short sessions? (Catalog 1: reusable co-op patterns)

### Takeaway
The patterns with the best evidence for mixed-skill, same-room, own-device play are: asymmetric roles with information asymmetry (each phone shows something different, so talking is the gameplay), time-boxed rounds scored by a shared score rather than lives, shared-control of one "vehicle", rescue/revive that costs the team nothing, generous assist/scaling options, and co-opetition where the team total is built from each player's placement. The patterns with the worst failure modes for siblings are tight physical coupling with no forgiveness (Pico Park rope levels, Chained Together) and pure chaos with time pressure and imprecise controls (Moving Out).

### Cited Findings

#### P1. Forced cooperation / shared goal ("nobody finishes unless everybody does")
- Pico Park (2016/2019/2021): "The rule is quite simple: 'Get all the keys and get to the goal and clear', but all 48 levels have special gimmicks designed specifically for multiplayer… every level requires cooperation to complete"; 2–8 players; a Battle Mode exists "once co-op play is completed" — [Pico Park official description via Vaporlens mirror](https://vaporlens.app/app/1509960/pico_park.md)
- Pico Park level-set structure includes "Go together — All players are connected to each other by a rope, causing the movement of any one player to affect all other players", "Time limit", "Gimmick — each character has a special function" — [Wikipedia: Pico Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pico_Park)
- Design reason it works (critical-analysis blog, Apr 2026): "unlike cooperative games where one skilled player can carry the team, here everyone matters equally, which forces genuine cooperation (and frustration)"; the challenge "comes from communication and cooperation" rather than controls — [Mechanics of Magic, Critical Play: Competitive Analysis (Pico Park), 16 Apr 2026](https://mechanicsofmagic.com/2026/04/16/critical-play-competitive-analysis-122/)
- Failure mode: Steam-review summaries report "the need for coordination can result in frustration or even 'trolling' behavior, especially with more players", and "intense frustration, anger, and stress, often leading to arguments" — [Vaporlens Pico Park review summary](https://vaporlens.app/app/1509960/pico_park); a sibling play report: the author "first played it on PC with my little sister — luckily, she is the more cooperative of my two siblings" — [Daily Campus, "Destroy your friendships with Pico Park", 14 Nov 2023](https://dailycampus.com/2023/11/14/the-backlog-destroy-your-friendships-with-pico-park/)
- Chained Together (June 2024): "up to four players are connected by a chain and must reach the top of the map… players can find ten power-ups called Wings that allow them to recover from falling. Players may choose a hard mode, where lava constantly rises, or an easy mode, where there are checkpoints"; 80,000 players in first week — [Wikipedia: Chained Together](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chained_Together)
- Chained Together reviewer: "what Chained Together does well when compared to other rage games is making sure the experience is still fun… bringing your friends into the mix can turn your anger into laughter… Trauma bonding is a great way to connect with your friends but be wary that it could destroy friendships as well" — [Daily Campus, 12 Nov 2024](https://dailycampus.com/2024/11/12/the-backlog-ascend-with-friends-in-chained-together/)
- Academic framing of the same lever: "closely coupled" patterns "provided a more enjoyable experience but introduced additional challenges in case of a lack of communication" (remote play study, APCHI 2012, abstract) — [Beznosyk et al., Hasselt University repository](https://documentserver.uhasselt.be/handle/1942/13935); in casual games "closely-coupled collaboration, where one player's actions are directly influenced by the other(s), remains limited… the need to coordinate actions may be one reason" (ICEC 2012, abstract) — [Beznosyk et al., Hasselt University repository](https://documentserver.uhasselt.be/handle/1942/14230)

#### P2. Asymmetric roles / information asymmetry (talking is the mechanic)
- Keep Talking and Nobody Explodes (2015): "one player as the 'Defuser', playing the game on a device (supporting both keyboard and mouse, touchscreen and gamepad…), and the remaining players as the 'Experts' reading the provided bomb defusal manual. As designed, the Defuser cannot look at the manual… the Experts cannot see the bomb… communicate… directly from across a table, or online" — [Wikipedia: KTANE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keep_Talking_and_Nobody_Explodes)
- Designer's framing (GDC 2016, Ben Kane, Steel Crate): "at its heart is a game about communication between players… puzzles both inside and outside of the 'game' to keep players talking in ways that encourage tension, mistakes, hilarity, and occasionally even camaraderie… balance approachability and challenge… inspiration from board games" — [GDC Vault session page](https://gdcvault.com/play/1023113/Designing-Asymmetric-Gameplay-For-Keep)
- Operation: Tango (2021, Clever Plays): "Verbal communication is at the heart of the experience, with progression tied to the dialogue between friends. Only by sharing the unique perspective of each role can players overcome the many challenges"; developer notes it was "like building two games in one", and that emergent fun includes "the hacker trolling the agent by activating horrendous elevator music" — [PlayStation Blog, 15 Jun 2020](https://blog.playstation.com/2020/06/15/co-op-spy-thriller-operation-tango-explores-innovations-in-asymmetrical-co-op/); the studio deliberately offers no couch co-op/split-screen because local play would undermine each player having a separate role (per studio FAQ as summarised in search results) — [Co-Optimus article](https://www.co-optimus.com/article/16542/operation-tango-is-an-asymmetrical-co-op-spy-thriller.html)
- Overcooked role splits (Ghost Town Games, 2016): "we made sure there were generally always more actions to perform than players available… players couldn't simply perform one action for the whole level, they would have to split their time… and communicate"; "we added delays… stay with the pot, or start preparing for the next order and risk the pot catching fire"; "most crucially… disruptions: events which would change the rules midway through a level forcing players to rethink their strategy and importantly, to communicate" (sliding counters, earthquake splitting the kitchen) — [Game Developer, "Game Design Deep Dive: Building truly cooperative play in Overcooked", 26 Aug 2016](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/game-design-deep-dive-building-truly-cooperative-play-in-i-overcooked-i-)
- Lovers in a Dangerous Spacetime (Asteroid Base): biggest design struggle "was finding ways to encourage players to take on different roles… colour-coded power-ups… didn't end up working"; solution: "We'll just let people gravitate to the roles they want to play… 'I have no interest in doing anything but this'"; advice: watch players' faces at shows — "their normal relationship dynamics collide with your artificial systems" — [Game Developer, "Co-op communication in Lovers in a Dangerous Spacetime"](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/co-op-communication-in-i-lovers-in-a-dangerous-spacetime-i-)
- Classic design-pattern definition: "Asymmetric Abilities — Players, or game elements, do not all have the same actions available… class-based multiplayer first-person shooters encourage co-operation between players by giving them Asymmetric Abilities that can be put to most efficient use by coordinating actions" — [Björk & Holopainen pattern (mirror)](https://math.vu.nl/~eliens/demo/pattern-asymmetricabilities.html)
- Parent guidance for mixed skill: "Give the kid the 'easier' role in asymmetric games. Portal 2's role rotation, Split Fiction's chapter choices, It Takes Two's character swap. Let your kid pick the role that sounds most fun to them. It is usually the right one for their skill level" — [Two Average Gamers, Age-by-Age Co-Op Guide (2026)](https://www.twoaveragegamers.com/age-by-age-co-op-guide-kids/)

#### P4. Relay and baton-pass
- Video-game relays exist mainly as sports mini-games: in Mario & Sonic at the Olympic Games (2007) "in relay events, such as the 4 × 100 metres relay and 4 × 100 metres aquatics relay, players can assemble teams comprising any four characters" — [Wikipedia: Mario & Sonic at the Olympic Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mario_%26_Sonic_at_the_Olympic_Games)

#### P7. Co-op vs the clock / vs AI rivals
- Overcooked replaced lives with scoring because with "3 lives… there was a general sense from players that they were constantly failing and that they were just doing their best to prolong the round which detracted from their sense of unity" — [Game Developer deep dive, 2016](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/game-design-deep-dive-building-truly-cooperative-play-in-i-overcooked-i-); the founder on the kitchen analogy: "it's this feeling that you're all in it together, even if you're all shouting at each other… you are working against the clock" — [MCV/Develop post-mortem, 26 Jun 2017](https://mcvuk.com/the-develop-post-mortem-overcooked/)
- Left 4 Dead's AI "Director": "Instead of fixed spawn points for enemies, the Director places enemies and items in varying positions and quantities based upon each player's current situation, status, skill and location" — [Wikipedia: Left 4 Dead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Left_4_Dead); E3 2008 demo showed it easing off when a four-player group struggled with ammo and accuracy — [Destructoid, E3 08](https://destructoid.com/?p=29615)

#### Evidence on why co-op suits tweens/siblings (less toxic than PvP, bridges skill gaps)
- Longitudinal, 194 children aged 7.3–11.4: "competitive gaming was associated with decreases in prosocial behavior, but only among children who played video games with high frequency" (≈8 h+/week); cooperative gaming was not associated with prosocial change — [Lobel et al. 2017, Tilburg University](https://research.tilburguniversity.edu/en/publications/video-gaming-and-childrens-psychosocial-wellbeing-a-longitudinal-/)
- Experiment, 4–6-year-olds, 5 minutes of a two-player game: children who played cooperatively liked their partner more than those in the competitive condition — [ECNU Journal of Psychological Science, 2018](https://jps.ecnu.edu.cn/EN/Y2018/V41/I2/364)
- 2025 experiment, 120 kindergartners: "playing a cooperative game (versus competitive game) increased sharing behavior", with a gender interaction — [PMC, 2025](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12268353/)
- Adolescent friend dyads study (2019) examined whether competitive vs cooperative play changed friendship quality and prosocial behaviour; reviewer stresses social context of play is usually overlooked — [Take This research review, Jul 2021](https://takethis.org/2021/07/research-review-influence-of-competitive-and-cooperative-video-games-on-behaviour-during-play-and-friendship-in-adolescence)
- Harris (PhD thesis 2019): "Playing games with preexisting social relations, such as family and friends, has been shown to help strengthen relationships, but it can be difficult to find games that… accommodate the wide variety of player types, ability levels, genre preferences, and social roles… Asymmetric cooperative games… are well-positioned to tackle this multi-faceted problem by providing different players with different interfaces, challenges, abilities, and information while tightly coupling their interactions through shared goals and feedback" — [UWSpace thesis record](https://uwspace.uwaterloo.ca/items/3aa63daa-5ba1-4ed7-ba64-813857472cbb/full)
- Parent-practice advice: "Do not grab the controller to 'fix' their mistakes… Celebrate their micro-wins"; "the co-op sweet spot is roughly age 6 to 12" — [Two Average Gamers (2026)](https://www.twoaveragegamers.com/age-by-age-co-op-guide-kids/); sibling-rules: "You are on the same team. The game is the enemy, not your brother"; "Rotation of Roles" — [Screenwise guide](https://screenwiseapp.com/guides/couch-co-op-games-that-bring-families-together)

### Inferences
Each pattern below gets a one-sentence "how it would look with horses and jumps" (inference, not sourced) and a fit note for 2–4 mixed-skill players on own touch devices in short sessions.
- P1 Forced cooperation: a course only "counts" when every rider is through the finish; a clear round by the weakest rider is worth as much as the strongest's. Fit: high, *if* forgiveness is built in (checkpoints/re-present a fence, no lava-style wipe); the Pico Park/Chained Together evidence shows raw forced coupling turns into blame between siblings.
- P2 Asymmetric roles: one phone is "the rider" (taps for take-off timing/steering), the other is "the trainer/groom/course-walker" who sees the course plan, stride counts or the horse's energy and must call "three strides… now!" — each device shows different information, so talking across the room is the game. Fit: best match for same-room/own-device; also the cleanest way to bridge skill (give the younger sibling the role they like; rotate roles each round per Lovers' "let people gravitate").
- P3 Shared control of one horse: one player steers/line, the other controls pace and take-off (Double Dash driver/item-thrower split; Shared RC left/right split). Fit: high for 2 players, novel for horse games; risk is control latency between devices and "who caused the refusal" arguments.
- P4 Relay: each rider jumps a section of the course then hands over in a changeover box (FEI 268 "forefeet on the ground" rule becomes a timing window). Fit: very high — it is the native equestrian team format, lets every skill level jump its own height, and naturally structures a 60–90 s session. Risk: weak rider "costs" the team time — fix with handicap heights per rider.
- P5 Synchronized actions: pairs jump a fence side by side (hunter-trials "within 20 m" rule) or must land within a shared timing window to score a "pair bonus". Fit: medium — satisfying with 2, hard to read on separate screens; needs a strong shared visual/audio cue.
- P6 Rescue/revive: a rider who refuses/falls becomes a "loose horse"; a teammate rides alongside to catch it (touch) and both continue with no team penalty (Mario Wonder ghost model). Fit: high; cheap to implement; it turns the weakest player's failures into a teammate's hero moment.
- P7 Co-op vs clock/AI: team vs the time allowed and vs a CPU "rival team" whose scores fill empty slots (MK World CPU fill) — so 2 players still get a full Nations Cup. Fit: high; essential for short sessions and variable player counts.
- P8 Co-opetition: team total is the sum of each rider's placing/faults (MK World scoring), while each player also sees their personal best round/time. Fit: high for 10–11-year-olds who already know ribbons and placings; keeps a parent engaged without "beating" the kids.
- P9 Drop-in/drop-out: a parent can join as the 3rd/4th team member mid-session or leave; their slot becomes a CPU rider. Fit: high; browser/own-device makes join-by-link natural.
- P10 Scaling: fence heights, time allowed and number of fences scale with player count and a per-rider "height class" (PC70/PC80-style) so siblings jump different heights on the same course. Fit: high; mirrors how real Pony Club teams mix heights.
- P11 Own device: adopt Spaceteam/KTANE's model — every phone shows a different panel (rider view vs course plan vs stopwatch), and the room's voice is the network. Fit: the strongest design constraint-turned-feature for this project.
- P12 Chaos-as-fun: slapstick horse physics (spooks, loose horses, flying poles) with harmless failure and reset. Fit: medium — kids who ride real horses may want their horses respected; keep chaos on the environment (wind, weather, mischievous ponies), not on the rider's competence.
- P13 Rhythm co-op: canter rhythm as a shared beat; both players tap the take-off beat in turn (DON-chan Band). Fit: medium-high as a *sub-mechanic* for take-off timing; "rhythm-only" games age out quickly.

## Key Question 2 — What does design research say about player "coupling", asymmetry, and what makes forced cooperation fun instead of frustrating?

### Takeaway
The CHI/CHI PLAY line of work (Harris & Hancock 2015–2019; Moriarty et al. 2023) converges on: tight coupling is what makes co-op feel social, asymmetry raises connectedness/engagement, and the variables that convert coupling from frustrating to fun are *direction of dependence, timing of the cooperative action, necessity, and leadership/primacy* — plus making sure each role has enough to do and can see the effect of its actions on the other player.

### Cited Findings
- Harris & Hancock, CHI 2019 ("To Asymmetry and Beyond!"): two studies with the prototype Beam Me 'Round, Scotty! 2 — "the first comparing symmetric cooperative play (e.g., where players have the same interface, goals, mechanics, etc.) to asymmetric cooperative play (e.g., where players have differing roles, abilities, interfaces, etc.) and the second comparing the effect of increasing degrees of interdependence… results not only indicate that asymmetric cooperative games may enhance players' perceptions of connectedness, social engagement, immersion, and comfort with a game's controls, but also demonstrate how to further improve these outcomes via deliberate mechanical design changes, such as changes in cooperative action timing and direction of dependence" (DOI 10.1145/3290605.3300239) — [University of Waterloo Touchlab record](https://uwaterloo.ca/touchlab/references/asymmetry-and-beyond-improving-social-connectedness)
- Harris PhD thesis (Waterloo, 2019): "The first study established several characteristic dynamics of asymmetric cooperative play including considerations of directional dependence, synchronicity, necessity, leadership and primacy… a second study demonstrating that, even when controlling for visual and narrative aesthetic details, asymmetric cooperative play is perceived as more socially engaging than symmetric cooperative play" — [UWSpace](https://uwspace.uwaterloo.ca/items/3aa63daa-5ba1-4ed7-ba64-813857472cbb/full)
- Earlier papers in the line: "Beam Me 'Round, Scotty!": Studying Asymmetry and Interdependence in a Prototype Cooperative Game (CHI PLAY 2015); Leveraging Asymmetries in Multiplayer Games: Investigating Design Elements of Interdependent Play (CHI PLAY 2016); Beam Me 'Round, Scotty! II: Reflections on Transforming Research Goals into Gameplay Mechanics (CHI PLAY 2018) — [UWSpace record of BMRS II](https://uwspace.uwaterloo.ca/items/0e4264de-e962-499c-afaf-bb255f7fb95a/full); [Games Institute PDF](https://uwaterloo.ca/games-institute/sites/default/files/uploads/documents/johnharris_beam-me-round-scotty.pdf)
- RaceTrap, CHI PLAY 2023 Companion (Moriarty, Perriman, Rutledge, Taylor, Graham, Queen's University): defines coupling "as the manner in which players are involved and occupied with each others' play. Coupling captures the ways in which players see and are affected by other players' actions, and their awareness of other players' state and intentions. Coupling can be weak (players mainly do their own thing) or tight (players are engaged in close and continuous interaction)"; "greater coupling in a multiplayer game is what creates a more fun and engaging experience"; "If a given role has too many tasks to perform, it may be overwhelming, while if a role has too few tasks, then players' enjoyment may be impacted by their lack of control over the results of the game" — [PDF](https://equis.cs.queensu.ca/~equis/pubs/2023/moriarty-chiplay-2023.pdf)
- RaceTrap's concrete fix for weak coupling: originally "the Designer was able to spend time placing obstacles on an empty track the Driver wouldn't see until the next round… Early playtesting revealed that this initial weak coupling negatively impacted enjoyment"; so static obstacles ("the Designer had little reason to monitor the Driver… a wasted opportunity for interaction") were replaced by "triggered items which the Designer can activate by clicking on them, such as cannons and flamethrowers… Designers need to pay attention to what the Driver is doing… and Drivers now need to pay attention to where the Designer is" — [PDF](https://equis.cs.queensu.ca/~equis/pubs/2023/moriarty-chiplay-2023.pdf); game context: "one player races around a track that the other player is designing and triggering in real-time" — [Queen's School of Computing, 22 Sep 2023](https://www.cs.queensu.ca/news/2023/09/22/videogame-by-qsc-graduates-showcased-at-the-cne/)
- Coupling in casual games (Hasselt University): closely-coupled patterns gave "a more enjoyable experience but introduced additional challenges in case of a lack of communication" (APCHI 2012); "introducing close coupling in the casual games under consideration is a potential way to increase the player experience" (ICEC 2012) — [APCHI record](https://documentserver.uhasselt.be/handle/1942/13935); [ICEC record](https://documentserver.uhasselt.be/handle/1942/14230)
- Cooperative Performance Metrics (Seif El-Nasr et al., CHI 2010; Aghabeigi M.Sc. 2011): "a set of cooperative design patterns… and a set of validated performance metrics that can be used to gauge the users' experience in a cooperative game… applied… to evaluate four commercial cooperative games" — [SFU Summit](https://summit.sfu.ca/item/11576); CHI 2010 paper DOI 10.1145/1753326.1753363 — [CORE record](https://core.ac.uk/works/9553460)
- Earlier pattern catalogue: Rocha, Mascarenhas & Prada, "Game Mechanics for Cooperative Games" (Zon Digital Games 2008, pp. 72–80) reviews design patterns and challenge archetypes of co-op games — [GAIPS publication record](https://gaips.inesc-id.pt/component/gaips/publications/showPublication/3/87)
- Practitioner evidence on frustration drivers: in Overcooked, lives made players feel "constantly failing"; score fixed it — [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/game-design-deep-dive-building-truly-cooperative-play-in-i-overcooked-i-); in Lovers, forcing role assignment "didn't end up working, it was kind of confusing" — [Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/co-op-communication-in-i-lovers-in-a-dangerous-spacetime-i-); Moving Out's "inconsistent controls can strain relationships" (Game Informer via search summary) — [Game Informer](https://www.gameinformer.com/review/moving-out/moving-out-review-packed-with-frustration)

### Inferences
- "Forced" cooperation is fun when (a) each player can *see* the effect of their action on the partner within a second or two (RaceTrap's triggered items; Spaceteam's shouted orders), (b) dependence is bidirectional or rotates (Harris: direction of dependence), (c) failure is cheap and shared (Overcooked score, Mario Wonder ghost), and (d) roles are self-selected or rotated, not imposed (Lovers). It is frustrating when one player's mistake erases everyone's progress with no recovery (Chained Together hard mode, Pico Park rope levels) or when a role has too little agency (RaceTrap's early Designer).
- For a horse game on separate devices, the cheapest way to achieve tight coupling without physics-level chaos is *information asymmetry + a short shared timing window* (e.g., course-walker calls the stride, rider taps take-off) — this is exactly the "cooperative action timing" variable Harris & Hancock found tunable.
- "Necessity" (Harris) should be partial: the team can finish without the weaker player's perfect input, but scores much better with it — this preserves the weaker sibling's relevance without making them a liability.

### Gaps
- The full text of Harris & Hancock 2019 (effect sizes, specific manipulations of timing and direction) and of the Beznosyk papers was not retrievable (ACM/IFIP pages blocked); findings above are from abstracts and repository records.
- The CHI 2010 Cooperative Performance Metrics list (e.g., laughter, worked-out strategies, helping) was not retrieved verbatim; only the thesis abstract.
- No specific CHI PLAY paper titled "Beyond the Division of Labour" was found; the closest matches are the Harris/Hancock papers above.

## Key Question 4 — Which team/relay/pairs formats exist in real equestrian sport, and what are their rules? (Catalog 2 with game mappings)

### Takeaway
Equestrian sport already contains a full library of multiplayer formats with precise, gamifiable rules: Nations Cup (4 ride, best 3 count, two rounds), Pony Club team jumping (3–4 ride, best 3 count), FEI relay (2–3 riders, forefeet-on-the-ground handover, Table C), Pony Club baton/box relays (half-course each), head-to-head knock-out over mirrored courses, mounted-games baton rules (dropped baton must be retrieved by the donor), pairs hunter trials (stay within 20 m), and skill classes (puissance, six-bar, accumulator, top score, take-your-own-line, hit-and-hurry, fault-and-out, two-phase). Each maps cleanly onto a 2–4 player co-op mechanic.

### Cited Findings

#### Nations Cup (FEI Art. 264; "Prix des Nations")
- "The Nations Cup… is the official international team Competition… there must be no individual placing… The Competition takes place over two rounds, over the same course during the course of the same day… judged under Table A, both rounds against the clock"; 12 obstacles, 5* heights 1.45–1.60 m; "the best six (minimum) to eight (maximum) teams after the first round take part in the second round with four Athletes per team"; second-round order "in the reverse order of the total Penalties in the first round of the best three Athletes in each team"; "If two or more Athletes of a team, participating with four Athletes… are eliminated or retire, the whole team is eliminated"; "Classification of the teams not taking part in the second round is based on the total Penalties of the three best Athletes"; tie for first → "a jump-off in which one Athlete per team will participate. - Longines League of Nations variant (2023): "In the first round all four riders of the ten teams… will compete. Only the best 3 results will count. Next in the second round the top 8… with only three riders at the start, eliminating the drop out score in this round" — [Equnews, 1 Jun 2023](https://equnews.com/article/australia/concept-for-new-nations-cup-format-returns-to-two-rounds); [HorseSport, 2 Jun 2023](https://horsesport.com/horse-news/new-league-nations-format-devised/)

- Accumulator (Art. 269): "six, eight or ten obstacles with an increasing difficulty… one point for obstacle number one not knocked down, two points for number two, three points for number three, etc. with a total of 21, 36 or 55 points. No point is awarded for an obstacle knocked down… may take place either with the first round against the clock without a jump-off, or… with a jump-off" — [FEI Jumping Rules 2025](https://inside.fei.org/sites/default/files/Jumping_Rules_2025_clean.pdf)
- Top Score (Art. 270): "Each obstacle carries from ten to 120 points according to its difficulty… must be built so that they can be jumped in both directions… A fixed time of 45 seconds (minimum) to 90 seconds (maximum)… the Athlete may jump any of the obstacles they wish in any order and in any direction" — same source
- Take-Your-Own-Line (Art. 271): "the obstacles may be jumped only once in the order chosen by the Athlete. Any Athlete who does not jump all the obstacles is eliminated… Athletes may cross the starting line and finishing line in either direction… under Table C… If the Athlete has not completed their course within 120 seconds… eliminated" — same source; club version: "5 seconds added for each knock down" — [Beechwood schedule via search summary](https://www.myridinglife.com/eventdetails.aspx?id=497130)
- Fault-and-Out (Art. 266): "The round finishes at the first Fault committed of whatever nature… bonus points are awarded: two points for an obstacle jumped correctly and one point for an obstacle knocked down"; Hit-and-Hurry (Art. 267): "instead of being eliminated at the first Fault, the Athlete gets two points for an obstacle correctly jumped and one point for an obstacle knocked down… fixed time of 60 to 90 seconds (45 seconds indoors)… the Athlete who at the end of the fixed time has acquired the greatest number of points in the fastest time" wins — same source

#### Speed, jump-off, knock-out, two-phase
- Table C (Art. 239): "Faults under Table C are penalised in seconds… Obstacle knocked down… Four seconds (three seconds for the second phase of two-phase Competitions, for knock-out Competitions and for any jump-off under table C) for outdoor Competitions; Three seconds for indoor"; first disobedience with knock-down → "time correction of six seconds" — same source
- Hunting / Speed and Handiness (Art. 263): "judged under Table C… Courses must be twisting, with obstacles very varied (alternative obstacles allowed, giving the Athlete the opportunity to shorten their track, but by taking a more difficult obstacle)" — same source
- Jump-off (Art. 238 / 245): competitions against the clock — "Athletes with equality of Penalties for any place are placed in accordance with the time taken… In the event of equality of Penalties and time for first place, there may be a jump-off over a shortened course over obstacles, which may be increased in height and/or spread"; "Only Athletes who are in equal first place… may take part in a jump-off… must start the same Horse" — same source; general explanation: "the jump-off has fewer jumps than the first round but is usually much more difficult… the penalties for knocking a pole down is four per pole" — [Wikipedia: Show jumping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Show_jumping)
- Knock-Out (Art. 272): "takes place by pairs of Athletes against each other… The two Athletes will compete against each other simultaneously over two identical courses… If one Athlete enters the other Athlete's course and… interferes… eliminated. The winners of each eliminating round are qualified to compete in groups of two in the next eliminating round and so on until the two finalists meet"; Annex III gives the 16- and 8-athlete bracket seeding (1st vs 16th, 8th vs 9th…) — [FEI Jumping Rules 2025](https://inside.fei.org/sites/default/files/Jumping_Rules_2025_clean.pdf)
- Two-Phase (Art. 274): "two phases run without interruption… The first phase is a course of seven to nine obstacles… The second phase takes place over four to six obstacles… Athletes penalised in the first phase are halted by ringing the bell… Athletes not penalised in the first phase continue the course" — same source; Two Rounds (Art. 273): "two courses, identical or different… Each Athlete must participate with the same Horse" — same source

#### Eventing team scoring
- Olympic eventing since Tokyo: "the four-person-team format, which saw the highest score dropped in each phase, was done away with. It was replaced with a three-member team — and no dropped score. Instead, teams have three opportunities to make a substitution… at the cost of 20 penalties"; eliminated pairs continue with 200 penalties per phase not completed; "Abuse of Horse or Dangerous Riding" elimination bars substitution — [Eventing Nation, Jul 2024](https://eventingnation.com/how-is-eventing-scored-in-the-olympics/); older format (e.g., 1984): "scores from the top 3 horse and rider pairs for each nation were summed… the lowest pair's score was dropped" — [Wikipedia: Equestrian at the 1984 Summer Olympics – Team eventing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equestrian_at_the_1984_Summer_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Team_eventing)

#### Chase-Me-Charlie
- Only one description found: a riding-school show's final class "referred to as 'Chase Me Charlie,' in which riders had to jump a single fence that increased in height after every successful attempt" — [Cayman Compass, 19 Dec 2017 (via search summary; page blocked to fetch)](https://www.caymancompass.com/2017/12/19/cayman-riding-school-holds-christmas-horse-show/)

### Inferences — mapping each format to a 2–4 player co-op mechanic (horse version in one line)
- Nations Cup / Pony Club team (4 ride, best 3 count, two rounds): the canonical "co-opetition with a drop score" — 2–4 players each jump the same course; the worst round is discarded, so the weakest sibling can never sink the team, and a CPU "rival nation" fills empty slots. Rationale: directly mirrors what these kids see at shows; drop score is the built-in skill-gap bridge.
- Olympic 3-rider / LLN second round (no drop score): a "pressure round" variant for experienced trios where everyone counts — unlock later.
- FEI relay (2–3 riders, consecutive course, forefeet-on-ground handover, Table C, 4th disobedience or any fall eliminates the team): a sequential relay where each player's phone goes "live" when the previous rider lands off the last fence — the handover is a timing window (go too early = team penalty, Cheshire's 10-second rule). Fits short sessions: one relay ≈ 60–90 s.
- Pony Club half-course baton / box relay: split the course between two players; the "box" (ground-pole square) is a literal on-screen zone the partner must enter before the next rider may start — easiest relay to read on separate devices.
- Cheshire inter-branch relay (two teams in the ring at once on mirrored courses; fall → next rider completes the course for them): team-vs-team relay with *rescue baked in* — if a rider falls, the next teammate must finish their section first (a natural rescue/carry mechanic).
- Olympia Pairs Relay / Mini-Major (kid + pro pairs): "mixed pairs" where the parent (or stronger sibling) is the "international rider" partnered with the Pony Club kid — makes the skill gap the premise, not the problem.
- Knock-Out (two riders simultaneously over identical mirrored courses; interference = elimination): head-to-head 2v2 relay brackets on split courses — co-op inside a bracket; also the best "spectator" format for a 4-player room.
- Pairs hunter trials (stay within 20 m; 20 penalties per breach): a synchronized-riding mechanic — two players ride side by side and are penalised when they separate; no "dressing fence" needed. Risk: needs a very clear on-screen distance cue.
- Mounted games baton rules (dropped baton must be retrieved by the donor, dismounted; receiver can't be swapped; 5 m line): a baton object with physics-like failure that is recoverable — "drop" costs time, not elimination. Pony Pairs rope race: two players must keep a rope taut (both must stay in sync) — a 2-player synchronized mini-game.
- Pony Express: ordered pick-up of coloured letters behind a line — a relay with an asymmetric "postman" role (a non-riding player hands items) — ideal for the parent or a younger sibling on a phone.
- Puissance / Six Bar / Chase-Me-Charlie (single fence or line raised each round, successive jump-offs, no time): team "how high can we go" — the team survives while any member clears; everyone gets the next height only if the team clears; natural 4-round session cap (FEI stops after four jump-offs).
- Accumulator (1,2,3… points, nothing for a knockdown) and Top Score (10–120-point fences, any order, 45–90 s): team points races where each player chooses fences by confidence — the weaker rider farms low-point fences, the stronger goes for the 120-point oxer; co-opetition with built-in self-handicapping.
- Take-Your-Own-Line (jump every fence once, own order, 120 s cap, Table C): a planning co-op — one player (course-walker role) draws the line on their device, the rider rides it; or two riders split the fence set between them.
- Hit-and-Hurry / Fault-and-Out (fixed 45–90 s, 2 points clear / 1 point knockdown): the ideal "short session" scoring — a team round is always ≤90 s and a knockdown still scores.
- Two-Phase (clean first phase unlocks phase two): a gate mechanic — the team only enters the jump-off phase if the first phase is clear (use the best-3 rule so one error doesn't block).
- Eventing team scoring (3 count, substitution costs 20): drop-in/drop-out formalised — swapping a player mid-session is allowed but costs the team a small penalty, so sessions stay fair.
- Jump-off: the natural tie-break and "one more go" hook — a shortened, raised course against the clock; for co-op, one team member (chosen by the team, FEI 264.9.2.1) rides the jump-off while the others watch — an explicit leadership/primacy decision in Harris's terms.

### Gaps
- Hickstead "relay" rules: no relay class rules found at Hickstead; only the NSEA schools team championship (single-phase team of four). The task's "Hickstead/Olympia relays" is only confirmed for Olympia (Pairs Relay, Mini Pairs Relay, Mini-Major).
- Pas de deux / hunter pairs (US "hunter pairs abreast") rules not found; only UK hunter-trial pairs (20 m rule).
- Chase-Me-Charlie: no governing-body rule text found; the only source is a 2017 newspaper description.
- USPC 2026 Mounted Games rulebook could not be opened (download returned an HTML stub); baton rules are from search summaries of that document. The Pony Club Australia MG rulebook PDF was image-based and could not be text-extracted.
- FEI "Masters" (Art. 262.4) text was truncated in extraction.

## Key Question 5 — What co-op features do kids ask for in horse games (shared stable, riding side by side, helping each other, trading)?

### Takeaway
Direct kid requests could not be retrieved from forums (Reddit blocked); the strongest available signals are (a) what horse games advertise as their social hook — ride together, chat, riding clubs, challenge each other — and (b) what kids and parents complain about: toxic public chat, moderation and cost. The critical rant that The Mane Quest amplified ("Horse Games Are Trash") argues that quality of the riding itself, not feature count, is what this audience is missing.

### Cited Findings
- Star Stable's own pitch of social play: "Meet up with your friends and ride together, chat or challenge each other in one of the island's many competitions. Or why not start your own riding club?" — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/star-stable-online-horse-game/id1445695988); riding clubs "can be used to plan trail rides, picnics, and parades" (search summary of Star Stable material) — [Star Stable: information for parents](https://www.starstable.com/en/parents)
- Kids' complaints (528 kid reviews summarised): "high costs for membership and in-game currency… an increasingly toxic in-game community and inadequate chat moderation" — [Common Sense Media](https://www.commonsensemedia.org/game-reviews/star-stable)
- Horse Riding Tales' pitch: "ride your horse with all your friends as you set out on adventures" and live chat with online friends (App Store description via search summary) — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/US/app/id1442304261)
- A widely shared r/Games rant on horse-game quality ("Horse Games Are Trash and I'm Over It") was endorsed by The Mane Quest, the main horse-game criticism site — [The Mane Quest, 12 Feb 2020](https://www.themanequest.com/blog/2020/2/12/recommended-horse-games-are-trash-and-im-over-it)
- Minecraft mod demand for shared riding: "DualHorse" lets a second player "right-click on their horse to join them" (two players on one horse) — [SpigotMC resource (via search summary)](https://speedgot.ajg0702.us/resources/99663); a "Let's Ride Together (Two players on one horse)" mod also exists — [Moddex listing](https://moddex.gg/mod/ride-together-two-players-on-one-horse)
- Parent-side rules that work for sibling co-op: "Pause and Breathe", "Rotation of Roles", "You are on the same team. The game is the enemy" — [Screenwise guide](https://screenwiseapp.com/guides/couch-co-op-games-that-bring-families-together)

### Inferences
- "Ride together" is table stakes in horse games, but it is *parallel* play; nothing found offers interdependent play (one rider's action changing another's outcome). A co-op jumping game can be the first to make riding together consequential.
- The existence of "two players on one horse" mods for Minecraft is weak but real evidence of demand for shared-mount play (Pattern P3).
- Because the loudest kid complaints concern public-chat toxicity and paywalls, the safest and most differentiated design is: private rooms, no text chat (voice is the room), and no pay-to-win.

### Gaps

## Shortlist — the 8–10 most promising co-op mechanics for a 2–4 player browser show-jumping game (one-line rationale and risk each)

### Takeaway
Lead with the formats the audience already knows from the show ring (team round with drop score, relay with handover window, puissance ladder) and make them interdependent with one information-asymmetric role per device; keep failure cheap (rescue, re-present, points-not-lives) and sessions ≤ 90 s per round.

### Cited Findings
- Grounding references for the shortlist are the sources above: FEI Art. 264/268/262/269/270/271/272 — [FEI Jumping Rules 2025](https://inside.fei.org/sites/default/files/Jumping_Rules_2025_clean.pdf); Pony Club best-3-of-4 — [PCUK SJ Rulebook 2026](https://resources.pcuk.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/RULEBOOK-Show-Jumping-2026-Web.pdf); Cheshire relay changeover/rescue rules — [PDF](https://branches.pcuk.org/eastcheshire/wp-content/uploads/sites/352/2024/05/Royal-Cheshire-Show-PC-Team-Relay-Rules-2024.pdf); asymmetry/coupling evidence — [Harris & Hancock 2019](https://uwaterloo.ca/touchlab/references/asymmetry-and-beyond-improving-social-connectedness), [RaceTrap 2023](https://equis.cs.queensu.ca/~equis/pubs/2023/moriarty-chiplay-2023.pdf); own-device talk mechanics — [Spaceteam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spaceteam), [KTANE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keep_Talking_and_Nobody_Explodes); points-not-lives and disruptions — [Overcooked deep dive](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/game-design-deep-dive-building-truly-cooperative-play-in-i-overcooked-i-); no-cost revive — [Nintendo, Mario Wonder tips](https://play.nintendo.com/news-tips/tips-tricks/super-mario-bros-wonder-multiplayer-tips/); team placement scoring with CPU fill — [Gamereactor, MK World 1.5.0](https://www.gamereactor.eu/mario-kart-world-150-update-out-now-online-and-local-knockout-tour-team-races-and-more-1664523/); assist/granular difficulty — [TheSixthAxis, Moving Out](https://thesixthaxis.com/2020/04/23/moving-out-review); frustration failure modes — [Vaporlens Pico Park summary](https://vaporlens.app/app/1509960/pico_park), [Daily Campus Chained Together](https://dailycampus.com/2024/11/12/the-backlog-ascend-with-friends-in-chained-together/)

### Inferences (the shortlist)
1. **Team round with drop score ("Mini Nations Cup")** — 2–4 riders jump the same course, best N−1 rounds count, CPU rival team fills slots. Rationale: the real format kids know; the drop score is the built-in skill-gap bridge. Risk: with 2 players there is nothing to drop — use a CPU teammate or switch to "all count" with handicap heights.
2. **Relay with a handover window (FEI 268 / box relay)** — each rider jumps a section; the next phone goes live when the previous horse lands off the last fence; early start = time penalty. Rationale: native equestrian team mechanic, 60–90 s rounds, sequential turns suit separate devices. Risk: waiting players disengage — give them a live job (call strides, hold the stopwatch) while waiting.
3. **Course-walker ↔ rider asymmetry (KTANE/Spaceteam model)** — one device shows the course plan, stride counts, horse energy; the other only rides; the walker must say "left, four strides, go". Rationale: strongest evidence base (Harris & Hancock; Spaceteam) and turns same-room voice into the network. Risk: the younger sibling may be stuck "only talking" — rotate roles every round and make the walker's calls score points.
4. **Loose-horse rescue (no-cost revive)** — a refusal/fall turns the rider into a loose horse a teammate catches by riding alongside; team keeps its score. Rationale: Mario Wonder's ghost revive proves zero-cost rescue keeps mixed-skill groups together. Risk: can be abused to skip hard fences — cap rescues per round or add a small time cost like Cheshire's 10 s.
5. **Points-not-lives rounds (Hit-and-Hurry / Accumulator / Top Score)** — fixed 45–90 s, 2 points clear / 1 point knockdown, or fence values 10–120 chosen freely. Rationale: Overcooked's lesson (lives feel like constant failure); real FEI classes; self-handicapping by fence choice. Risk: can devolve into parallel play — add a team multiplier for fences jumped in the called order or "both riders cleared the same fence".
6. **Puissance / Six-Bar / Chase-Me-Charlie team ladder** — one fence (or line) rises each round; the team advances while any member clears; stops after four jump-offs like the FEI. Rationale: instant drama, natural 4-round session, everyone cheers one rider. Risk: strongest player carries — require a different rider to attempt each height, or require two clears per height.
7. **Co-opetition scoreboard** — team total from each rider's placing/faults (MK World model) plus a personal-best panel per device. Rationale: 10–11-year-olds already think in placings and ribbons; keeps a parent engaged without "winning". Risk: siblings compare personal numbers more than the team total — show the team result first and larger.
8. **Pairs within 20 m (synchronized riding)** — two riders jump side by side and lose points when they separate (hunter-trials pairs rule); optional "pair bonus" for landing within the same beat. Rationale: the only real "together on course" format; good for 2 players. Risk: hard to perceive on separate screens — needs a loud, simple proximity cue (rope tension, shared heartbeat).
9. **Shared-horse control (Double Dash split)** — one player steers/line, the other controls pace and take-off. Rationale: novel for horse games; strong for 2 siblings; mirrors "two on one horse" mod demand. Risk: blame for refusals and device latency — keep it as a bonus mode with forgiving take-off windows.
10. **Knock-out 2v2 on mirrored courses** — two teams relay simultaneously over identical courses (Cheshire/FEI 272), interference = elimination. Rationale: the best 4-player same-room format (two watch, two ride; spectators can cheer with presets as in MK World). Risk: it's competitive between pairs — keep it opt-in after the co-op core.

Cross-cutting guardrails (from the evidence): keep every round ≤ 90 s (FEI fixed-time classes; Overcooked rounds); scale fence height per rider (Pony Club height classes; DRG-style per-player scaling); let players pick/rotate roles rather than assigning them (Lovers); allow drop-in/drop-out with a small penalty (eventing substitution = 20 penalties) and CPU fill (MK World); keep chaos in the environment, not in the controls (Moving Out's control complaints vs Human: Fall Flat's "never becomes frustrating").

### Gaps
- No playtest data exists for any of these mechanics with 10–11-year-old riders on touch devices; the shortlist is an inference from adult/console evidence and real-sport rules and should be validated with the cheapest possible prototype of #2 or #3 first.
